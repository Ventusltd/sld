## Choosing the right representation

For UK work, explicitly select and review IEC-oriented product data/macros. Rockwell's catalogue distinguishes IEC and NEMA product families; its EPLAN guide distinguishes IEC and NFPA schematic data. NEMA product designation, NFPA drawing style and IEC symbol conformity are separate attributes. A UL or IEC product certification does not certify the graphical symbol used to depict it.

Mechanical STEP/DWG models help with dimensions and panel layout. PCB symbols/footprints help with electronic pin mapping and board design. EPLAN macros can provide device functions and connection information. A wiring diagram can show individual conductors. None should automatically be substituted for a reviewed single-line representation.

## Acquisition decision

Use these portals to identify exact parts and link supporting evidence. For any proposed import, record manufacturer, manufacturer part number, distributor SKU, model provider, asset type/format, document revision/date, source URL, file hash, terminal mapping, and the actual licence/permission governing redistribution and modification.

TraceParts' [API terms](https://developers.traceparts.com/docs/api-terms-of-use) restrict external redistribution without consent. Provider marketing about free downloads or manufacturer ownership is not permission to mirror a library. Keep restricted assets in their permitted design workflow; do not put them into the public source catalogue without established rights.

Every production symbol still needs independent engineering review and the required approved real-project drawing issued after 2012. Distributor documents and CAD downloads support research; they do not replace that approval evidence.
